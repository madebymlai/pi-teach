# Exam roadmap

Read this when creating the first timeline or explicitly replanning. `Roadmap.md` holds the agreed schedule from onboarding through the relevant exam dates. Actual work, assistance, outcomes, and continuation belong in `learning-records/`. Ordinary teaching sessions leave the timeline unchanged.

## Build during onboarding

1. Establish the current calendar date and confirm the goal, assessment dates and format, study availability, days off, and constraints in `COURSE.md`. Ask one question at a time. If an exam date is missing or already past, clarify it before constructing a dated exam plan.
2. Read the syllabus and registered sources. Map assessable objectives to topics, prerequisites, source pages, and practice activities. Read any existing learning records to identify prior knowledge and unresolved gaps. Distinguish official scope from estimates based on exercise frequency; label missing source coverage.
3. Allocate the available study sessions through exam day. Sequence prerequisites before dependent work and reserve spaced review, independent checkpoints, exam-like practice with correction time, breaks, and buffer capacity. Schedule written, oral, and lab preparation around their actual requirements and dates. If the scope does not fit, show the trade-offs and obtain the student's choice before agreeing the plan.
4. Present a complete dated timeline for approval, using the structure below. Give each study session a time budget, recall focus, next lesson objective, and specific practice. Identify essential work and optional extras. Include planned rest, buffer, review, and exam days. Mark unknown source mappings explicitly rather than inventing references.
5. After the student agrees, save the timeline in `Roadmap.md`. The initial plan is complete when the period through the exam is accounted for, the workload fits the agreed availability, and checkpoints have observable success criteria.

## Structure

```md
# Roadmap

Agreed: <date>
Planning basis: [[COURSE]] and [[SOURCES]].

## Timeline

| Date | Time budget | Recall | Lesson / objective | Practice / checkpoint |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |

## Checkpoints

<Dates, tasks, time limits, permitted aids, and success criteria.>

## Planning assumptions and trade-offs

<Uncertainties, source gaps, protected prerequisites, and agreed scope choices.>

## Revisions

<Only explicit, agreed replanning decisions: date, reason, and affected future work.>
```

Link existing topics, lessons, and exact source tasks where available. For a lesson not yet created, specify its topic and objective; resolve its eventual numbered file through the topic group in `lessons/lessons.md` during teaching. Creating a lesson does not require editing the timeline. Availability and exam facts remain authoritative in `COURSE.md`; the timetable is their agreed allocation.

The timeline contains planned work. Keep completion checkboxes, actual scores, last-studied pointers, and daily progress summaries in learning records rather than adding them here.

## Explicit replanning

Trigger this when missed sessions, substantial prerequisite gaps, or changed exam facts or availability make the remaining timeline unworkable, or when the student requests a new plan. A single wrong answer can be handled within a session without rewriting the schedule.

1. Compare the remaining scheduled work with the current date, verified learning records, and available time. Explain the concrete mismatch.
2. Propose changes to future sessions, identifying protected prerequisites, review, and assessment practice, and the work being deferred or removed. Fit the confirmed time budget; ask before increasing study hours or changing the exam goal.
3. Obtain agreement before editing. If approval is pending, retain the current timeline and record only the work actually done.
4. Re-read `Roadmap.md`, revise the affected future rows and checkpoints consistently, and append a dated reason in Revisions. Preserve past scheduled rows and the actual learning history. Finish by stating the revised next session and the effect on exam preparation.
