# COURSE.md format

`COURSE.md` lives at the subject vault root. It grounds teaching in the student's reason for learning and the verified course requirements. Populate the installer scaffold during onboarding; create the same concise sections if the file is absent.

- **Goal:** the concrete outcome the student wants and why it matters.
- **Success criteria:** observable capabilities such as constructing an unfamiliar proof or implementing an algorithm under the assessment constraints.
- **Course facts:** syllabus, assessment format/dates, and source references. Label unconfirmed facts and estimates. Verify dates from current sources.
- **Constraints and availability:** realistic study time, commitments, and other limits.
- **Teaching preferences:** the student's explicit, lasting preferences for how to study.
- **Notation and conventions:** lecturer terminology and notation, including terms the learner has not mastered yet.
- **Out of scope:** explicitly excluded topics, agreed with the student.
- **Open questions:** unresolved facts affecting teaching or planning.

Keep one goal per subject workspace. Ask one onboarding question at a time; preserve unknowns until answered. Confirm substantive goal changes and consequential scope trade-offs before updating them. When learning changes the goal, also capture the shift in a learning record.

## Before every update

1. Read the current file and identify the changed course fact or explicit, lasting student preference. If neither changed, leave `COURSE.md` untouched. Availability constraints affecting future planning qualify; today's session budget belongs in its learning record.
2. Route other changes to their owner: teaching procedures to the skill, content corrections to the affected teaching material, study evidence to learning records, and technical decisions to project documentation when worth retaining. A correction to the tutor is not automatically a student preference; only record a preference when the student actually establishes one.
3. Amend the existing entry as a concise current fact, retaining relevant source dates and uncertainty. Check the diff: each added sentence must describe the changed course fact or preference, not narrate an intervention or duplicate another document. Preserve meaningful superseded evidence in its learning record rather than accumulating a course change log.

Study sequencing belongs in `Roadmap.md`; evidence of understanding belongs in `learning-records/`.
